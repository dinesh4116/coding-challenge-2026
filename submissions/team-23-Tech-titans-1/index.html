How the analysis works

A transparent, rule-based visual-scoring prototype. It is not a trained ML model, and every point it awards can be traced to a rule below.

Measured image features

Each image is decoded in your browser, drawn to a canvas (downscaled to at most 512 px on its longest side for consistent measurement) and analysed pixel by pixel:

  • Dimensions & aspect ratio: original pixel size, compared with standard placements (1:1, 4:5, 9:16, 16:9, 1.91:1, 2:3).
  • Brightness: mean relative luminance.
  • Contrast: standard deviation of luminance (RMS contrast).
  • Colourfulness: the Hasler & Süsstrunk colourfulness metric, plus mean saturation.
  • Dominant colours & distribution: colour quantisation into clusters and a 12-bin hue histogram.
  • Clarity: variance of the Laplacian (a standard sharpness measure).
  • Visual complexity: share of strong Sobel edges.
  • Text-like detail (approximate): share of small blocks with dense stroke-like transitions. It cannot tell text from other fine patterns, so it informs suggestions only and is not part of the score.

A score of 65 or more gives “Likely to Get Reach”. A lower score gives “Less Likely to Get Reach”. The thresholds are design heuristics drawn from common creative best practice. They were not fitted to outcome data.

Dataset matching

An image is linked to dataset rows only on an exact, case-insensitive match: the filename (without extension) must equal a value in a filename column, an ad ID (optionally prefixed ad_/ad-), or a creative ID. If the name matches two different records, no match is made. Images without a match are still analysed on their own.

Limitations. The tool doesn't read the ad's message, offer, audience, targeting, budget, brand or platform algorithm, and all of these strongly affect real reach. It reports no confidence percentages, accuracy figures or predicted reach numbers, because no model has been validated to produce them. Use the scores to compare creatives and decide what to A/B test.
